Paper 1 — Using Twitter to Predict Chart Position for Songs (Tsiara and Tjortjis, 2020)
1.	What did they study? 
a.	They studied the relationship between number of mentions of a song and its artist, as well as semantic (positive, negative and neutral) orientation of posts and the song's chart performance.
2.	What method did they use? 
a.	They used regression analysis algorithm which estimated the difference between the actual and predicted chart positions and the moderated results. To get the official Billboard Hot 100 chart data they developed a method that extracted the artist name, song title and rank, and save them as .json and .csv files. They used Twitter Search API to collect relevant Twitter Posts.
3.	What did they find? 
a.	There is a moderate correlation of 0.2917 between song mentions and chart performance. Meanwhile, the correlation between artist mentions and their success is little to none.

Paper 2 — Comparative Analysis of Naïve Bayes Classifier and Support Vector Machine in Sentiment Analysis of Social Media Data (Aprinastya et al., 2024)
1.	What did they study? 
a.	The study examines the performance of Naïve Bayes Classifier and Support Vector Machine algorithms in sentiment analysis of the user sentiment based on Google Play Store and App Store reviews of the popular Genshin Impact game.
2.	What method did they use?
a.	For preprocessing, they used tokenisation, stemming and Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) to enhance the data quality. They classified sentiments into positive, negative and neutral using TextBlob-assisted processes, and for the final sentiment analysis, they used Naïve Bayes Classifier and Support Vector Machine. 
3.	What did they find? 
a.	They found that NBC had 71% accuracy for multilingual data (English and Indonesian reviews) compared to 63% for SVM. They concluded that their research shows that language-based adaptations are needed in machine learning algorithms to support multilingual sentiment analysis. 
Paragraph 1 — what they studied, what data they used, what method they used (~75 words) 
Aprinastya et al. (2024) examined the performance of the Naïve Bayes Classifier and Support Vector Machine algorithms in sentiment analysis of multilingual user reviews for the popular Genshin Impact game on Google Play Store and App Store. Preprocessing techniques such as tokenisation, stemming and TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency) were used to enhance the data quality. Classification was done using both NBC and SVM algorithms to carry out the final comparison for the sentiment analysis. 
Paragraph 2 — what they found, and one sentence explaining why this is relevant to your project (~75 words) 
The study found that Naïve Bayes Classifier returns a better accuracy of 71%, while Support Vector Machine had an accuracy of 63%, making Naïve Bayes Classifier the ideal choice for multilingual sentiment analysis. This study provides guidance on selecting the appropriate classification algorithm for this project. The comparison between NBC and SVM provides evidence for choosing the most accurate classifier when applied to English-language social media data collected from Twitter and Reddit. 
Paper 3 — Comparison of Naïve Bayes Classifier and Support Vector Machine in Classifying Public Responses on Twitter (Millennianita, Athiyah and Muhammad, 2024) 
1.	What did they study? 
a.	The study analyses the public sentiment of Indonesian fans on Twitter regarding the Kim Garam (former member of LE SSERAFIM) bullying allegation case.
2.	What method did they use? 
a.	They use NBC and SVM classification text mining methods for processing textual data. Sentiment classification results were labelled either positive or negative. They used Google Colaboratory for crawling to retrieve useful data on the case which was stored in an xlsx file. Libraries like ‘tweppy’ for accessing Twitter API and ‘Pandas for data handling. Pre-processing steps like cleaning, tokenisation and stemming were used. 
3.	What did they find? 
a.	Based on the sentiment analysis, they found that the public sentiment for the case was positive as Kim Garam was found to be innocent of her accusations, which led to a shift in public support for her. They also found that the accuracy of the NBC was 93% and SVM was 97% making SVM the better classification algorithm for this context.   
Paragraph 1 — what they studied, what data they used, what method they used (~75 words)
 Millennianita, Athiyah and Muhammad (2024) examined the public sentiment of Indonesian fans on Twitter regarding the Kim Garam bullying accusation case. They collected Twitter data with the keywords ‘kim garam’, ‘bullying’, and ‘bullying case’ by extracting them using a Python web crawler in Google Colaboratory, using ‘tweppy’ and ‘Pandas’ libraries for accessing the Twitter API and data handling, respectively. The data was stored in a Microsoft Excel file and went through pre-processing steps, including cleaning, tokenisation and stemming.

The study found that the sentiment analysis result showed more positive comments on Twitter concerning the Kim Garam bullying accusation case.
